What’s included?

The ranking covers the 19 regular routes running on the October comparison weekday. The download also includes seasonal line 33, Amtrak shuttles and the combined school-booster record. Boosters averaged 64.8 boardings/hour, but the report groups them together rather than ranking each one.

A boarding is one time someone gets on a bus, so a person who transfers is counted twice. The 25.9 system average includes boosters and shuttles. It’s a reference, not a cancellation rule. Source: MTD FY2025–26 report, PDF pp. 2–3 ↗

IMPLEMENTATION

Before changing service

A proposal needs passenger counts, an operating cost estimate and a review of affected connections.

  1. 01
    Passenger counts

    Find out who boards where, which buses get crowded, and how many people transfer.

  2. 02
    Access and transfers

    Look at walking distances, safe crossings, accessibility, school and work times, and the first and last bus. Apply the relevant Title VI equity requirements.

  3. 03
    Operating costs

    Estimate paid vehicle and driver hours, lost fares, transfer changes and implementation costs.

  4. 04
    Rider consultation and a trial

    Publish the proposed timetable, consult riders and test the changes. Track missed connections, crowding and ridership.

Effect of a 20% frequency cutINTERVALS

Space 20% fewer departures evenly and the gaps grow 25%: 20 → 25 minutes, 30 → 37.5, and 60 → 75.

Choosing departures to remove and estimating cost savings require trip counts and operating schedules.
