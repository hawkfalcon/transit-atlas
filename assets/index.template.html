Data and methods

Weekday service from 10 am to 2 pm.
October 15, 2019 and October 15, 2026.

01 / Comparison dates

2019: MTD’s original feed for August 19–December 1, sampled on Tuesday, October 15. It has 46 route records, with 45 running that day: 24 regular lines, 3 Amtrak shuttles and 18 school/other extras. The standby record 1001 has no trips.

Today: the official August 17–November 29, 2026 feed, sampled on Thursday, October 15. It includes August’s cuts. Of 39 records, 38 run that day: 19 regular lines, 3 Amtrak shuttles and 16 school boosters. Seasonal line 33 has no October weekday trips.

Both dates are school-in-session weekdays. 2019 is our pre-pandemic baseline, not an all-time record. Some routes, including 12X, are more frequent today.

02 / Frequency calculation

We take the first boardable stop on each trip, sort departures by direction, and measure the gaps. Keep the gaps whose midpoint falls between 10 am and 2 pm, then take the median across directions. We show a route frequency when at least one direction has two departures in that window. Each direction’s own interval also needs two midday departures.

That number describes departures from the route’s terminals. Branches and journey times affect the service at other stops. Click a route to see each direction and the full range of gaps. Purple Limited routes have scheduled trips, but too few midday departures to give a regular interval. Dark dashes mark routes with no trips in the chosen timetable.

The counters: regular-line count covers the whole day. Midday departures count trip starts from 10:00 up to, but not including, 14:00, in both directions. School extras, Amtrak shuttles and on-demand service are outside those totals. The percentage change compares midday departures; it is not a change in annual hours, budget or ridership. Overlapping routes are counted separately.

03 / Service and funding data

Saving MTD uses MTD’s FY2025–26 report: July 1, 2025–June 30, 2026, before August’s cuts. Annual boardings and boardings per revenue hour come from the YTD columns on PDF pages 2–3. The 24 published fixed-route records add up to 4,524,968 boardings. The table ranks the 19 current regular lines; the download also retains seasonal, Amtrak and school-booster data.

Selecting departures to reduce requires recent passenger counts and estimates of operating costs. The ideas here are independent analysis. The UCSB fee option comes from MTD’s July 2026 staff proposal for a Spring 2027 student vote.

The Route overlaps option screens all 19 current regular lines with the current GTFS feed: route shapes within 25 metres of each other, the stops both routes serve, and the bus blocks that show whether one bus already runs both routes. Pairs that share a bus block are already interlined—6 and 11 also alternate 15-minute departures from Transit Center—so merging their line numbers would remove no vehicle or driver hour. The study list instead ranks pairs run as separate bus blocks over at least 10 km of shared corridor with at least five shared stops, led by 15X/24X and 20/19X. Pairs that only share a freeway alignment with no common stop (12X/15X and 7/15X) are excluded because riders cannot switch between them.

04 / Reference paths

In Today view, the default dashed routes are 2019 lines missing from the current timetable. Other bus routes still overlap some of those streets. The Wave also runs on demand in Goleta/Isla Vista and Carpinteria; its zones are outside this fixed-route map.

Select a 2026-only route while viewing 2019 and the dashes show its 2026 path. Seasonal line 33 is also marked as having no trips on the October comparison date.
